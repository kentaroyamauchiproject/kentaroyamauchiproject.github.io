Press & Background

Press Summary

MINDBEBOP creates Noise-Canceling Apps for Your Mind — a family of six complementary apps exploring how people interact with their thoughts and attention.

While much of the conversation about the attention economy focuses on external distractions, MINDBEBOP also addresses the thoughts that continue competing for attention, even after external stimulation has stopped.

Its tools offer different ways to reduce stimulation, reconsider thoughts, give them another time or place, explore them spatially, and navigate toward a chosen Mental Destination.

MINDBEBOP calls this broader approach Mental Background Detox and the interconnected architecture supporting it Mental Background OS.

Unlike software designed to maximize engagement, MINDBEBOP does not assume that more interaction is better. Its tools are designed to be available when useful, without becoming another continuous demand on attention.

MINDBEBOP is intentionally non-clinical. It is not a form of therapy, a diagnostic tool, or a replacement for professional mental health care.

More ways to interact with your mind. Not more things your mind has to do.

The Attention Economy — Inside and Outside

Money determines what we can afford. Time determines how long we have. Attention determines what we experience.

Discussions about the attention economy commonly focus on digital products, advertising, notifications, social media, and other external forces competing for human attention.

MINDBEBOP also explores another side of this relationship: the competition for attention that can take place among our own thoughts.

Responsibilities, memories, expectations, concerns, desires, ideas, and unfinished tasks can all remain mentally present while we are doing something else.

Some require immediate attention. Some require attention later. Others may not require any action at all.

Yet they can all compete for attention in the same moment.

MINDBEBOP uses the idea of an internal attention economy as a way of examining this everyday experience.

External and internal demands on attention can also reinforce one another.

Something we see online may generate a thought. That thought may generate an urge to check something. Checking something may introduce new information, which may generate further thoughts.

The difficulty is not necessarily the device, the information, or the thought itself. It can arise when these movements of attention repeatedly interfere with what we want to be doing or experiencing.

Digital Detox addresses part of this relationship by reducing external stimulation. MINDBEBOP extends the approach to include the thoughts that remain present.

Putting a device away and putting a thought aside are two different things.

Mental Background Detox

Mental Background Detox is MINDBEBOP's approach to reducing unnecessary mental noise and friction by offering different ways to interact with thoughts and attention.

MINDBEBOP uses the term Mental Background to describe thoughts that may remain active while attention is directed elsewhere.

Some of these thoughts may behave like Thought Noise — continuing to draw attention when a person would rather attend to something else.

Mental friction may arise when there is a difference between where a person's attention is, what they want to be doing, and what they are currently able to do.

Neither mental noise nor mental friction necessarily requires the elimination of a thought.

A thought may be given another perspective, another time, some distance, a visible place, or a possible path toward action.

It may also be left alone.

Mental Background Detox is not a fixed routine, a clinical intervention, or an attempt to maintain permanent mental silence.

Its purpose is to make different possibilities available when they may be useful.

Something can remain important without requiring your attention right now.

Mental Background, Thought Noise, and Mental Background Detox are conceptual terms used within MINDBEBOP. They are not intended to describe established neurological mechanisms or clinical classifications.

Mental Background OS

Mental Background OS is the underlying architecture connecting MINDBEBOP's apps, concepts, and interactions.

It is not an operating system that replaces the operating system on a user's device.

It is an interconnected environment in which thoughts can be represented, organized, revisited, explored, and connected to actions.

Each MINDBEBOP app has its own purpose and can be used independently.

When useful, the apps can also work together.

A thought can exist as a Thought Block in Thought Space. That Thought Block can connect to one or more sets of Mental Steps. Those Mental Steps can bring together different MINDBEBOP tools and actions in the real world.

Something meaningful discovered along the way can be preserved through MindAnchor and made available again in another situation.

These connections allow users to move between different forms of interaction without requiring every thought to follow the same process.

Mental Background Detox describes the approach and its intended benefit.

Mental Background OS describes the architecture that supports it.

Reduce. Navigate. Anchor.

MINDBEBOP describes three broad categories of interaction with thoughts and attention.

They represent complementary possibilities, not mandatory stages of a process.

Reduce

Reduce unnecessary demands on attention.

This may involve reducing external stimulation, creating temporary digital boundaries, reconsidering a thought, giving it another time, or giving attention somewhere else to go.

Reducing a thought's demand on attention does not necessarily mean removing the thought itself.

Navigate

Move from a Current Position toward a chosen Mental Destination.

Mental Navigation can involve exploring perspectives, considering possible directions, and taking small structured actions through Mental Steps.

The destination belongs to the user. The route can change as the situation changes.

Anchor

Preserve something meaningful from an arrival so it can be encountered again later.

MindAnchor offers an optional way to retain something that may be useful in a future situation.

It does not freeze a mental state, score a journey, or declare a thought permanently resolved.

A thought does not have to become Thought Noise. A Thought Block does not have to become a destination. Mental Navigation does not have to end with a MindAnchor.

The user decides which possibilities, if any, are useful.

Thought Space & Thought Furniture

Thought Space is a three-dimensional environment within MindBackyard where thoughts, tasks, projects, and ideas can be represented as persistent Thought Blocks.

Rather than representing thoughts exclusively through lists or conventional documents, Thought Space allows users to explore their spatial relationships.

Thought Blocks can be moved, approached, rotated, resized, and related to one another.

Their positions, sizes, and distances do not carry universal meanings. The spatial relationships belong to the user.

Spatial Mental Shelving

MINDBEBOP calls one of the underlying interactions Spatial Mental Shelving: making the act of consciously setting a thought aside spatial, so that it can have a place without necessarily remaining in immediate attention.

Thought Space extends beyond shelving. It can also be used for organization, planning, exploration, and experimentation.

Nested Thought Spaces

A Thought Block can contain another Thought Space.

This allows a thought, project, or area of life to have its own spatial environment, containing other Thought Blocks and Thought Furniture.

Spaces can exist within other spaces without requiring every thought to be organized into a hierarchy.

Thought Furniture

Thought Furniture consists of interactive objects within Thought Space that offer different ways of working with Thought Blocks.

Each piece is designed around a different kind of interaction.

The Workbench offers a guided way to work with a fear-based protective thought and explore possible responses or actions.

The Portal offers a way to explore thoughts from another temporal perspective.

Placing a Thought Block on or within a piece of Thought Furniture does not require the user to interact with it immediately.

The furniture remains available until the user decides to interact with it.

Connecting Thoughts to Possible Paths

A Thought Block can be associated with one or more saved sets of Mental Steps.

These associations allow possible routes to remain available without turning the thought itself into a task or obligation.

The user can explore a thought spatially, consider its relationships, and decide whether any further action is useful.

A thought can have a place without having a destination.

Mental Navigation

Mental Navigation is MINDBEBOP's concept for intentional movement from a Current Position toward a chosen Mental Destination.

A Mental Destination may be a state, a perspective, a transition, a decision, an action, or another point the user wants to reach.

Mental Navigation begins when the user decides there is somewhere they want to go from where they are now.

It does not require every thought to have a destination.

Mental Steps

Mental Steps are small, structured actions that can help the user move toward a Mental Destination.

They can include interactions with MINDBEBOP tools as well as actions in the real world.

Sets of Mental Steps can provide reusable structures for situations that may occur again.

Stateful Mental Steps

Stateful Mental Steps can use information about the current situation, including previous actions and where the user left off, to determine what comes next.

Adaptive Navigation

Adaptive Navigation allows the route toward a Mental Destination to change in response to the situation, rather than requiring a fixed sequence of actions.

The user may arrive, continue later, reconsider the route, or decide not to continue.

MindEntry is MINDBEBOP's central Mental Navigation tool.

Its public description is GPS for Your Mind.

MindAnchor

Something meaningful can remain after Mental Navigation reaches its destination.

It may be a thought, a realization, a perspective, a moment, or another reminder of where the mind arrived.

MindAnchor provides an optional way to preserve something from that arrival so it can be encountered again later.

MINDBEBOP describes this possibility as Bookmarks for Your Mind.

The purpose is not to measure progress, reproduce a previous mental state, or guarantee a particular future outcome.

It is to create a point of return.

Whether encountering an earlier MindAnchor changes a future mental journey is something MINDBEBOP continues to explore.

MindBlank & Software Engagement

Many digital products are designed to encourage continued interaction.

Common measures of engagement include visit frequency, session duration, content consumption, and user activity.

These measurements can help explain how people use software. They do not necessarily establish whether the software is serving a person's needs.

MINDBEBOP does not assume that spending more time with its apps is inherently better.

A useful interaction might last thirty seconds. It might involve an hour of exploration in Thought Space. It might involve sitting quietly with MindZoneOut without trying to accomplish anything.

It might involve not opening an app at all.

The distinction is not between short and long sessions. It is between interaction that serves the user and interaction encouraged for its own sake.

MindBlank

MindBlank reflects this different approach to software.

Rather than focusing exclusively on active interaction, MindBlank represents time spent away from active MINDBEBOP interaction in supported situations.

It does not attempt to determine what a person was thinking, whether their mind was literally blank, or whether they were using their time well.

It is not a score, a streak, or a performance target.

There is nothing the user needs to achieve with MindBlank.

Sometimes the most useful thing an app can do is allow you to stop using it.

MINDBEBOP is designed to be available when useful, rather than to become another continuous demand on attention.

The Six Apps

Each MINDBEBOP app offers a different way to interact with thoughts and attention. They can be used independently or together.

MindBackOut
App Blocker for Your Mind.

Uses Apple's Screen Time framework to create temporary boundaries around selected apps, including optional app blocking during supported Mental Steps.

MindZoneOut
An Offline Space for Your Mind.

Provides a deliberately low-stimulation environment in which users can spend time without being required to complete a task or achieve a particular mental state.

MindFlipOut
Thought-Flipping Notepad.

Allows users to write down a heavy thought and explore a lighter response or another perspective.

MindShoutOut
Thought-Offloading Notepad.

Gives a thought another time to return, offering a way to reduce the need to keep remembering it right now.

MindBackyard
Thought Block Playground.

Provides two different environments: Infinite Hallway, for open-ended movement without requiring a goal, and Thought Space, where thoughts can be represented, organized, explored, and connected to possible actions.

MindEntry
GPS for Your Mind.

The central Mental Navigation tool for MINDBEBOP, providing structured ways to move from a Current Position toward a chosen Mental Destination through Mental Steps.

These apps are presented in an order that moves from external demands on attention toward internal thought interaction, spatial exploration, and intentional mental movement. This is not a prescribed order of use.

A Typical MINDBEBOP Interaction

A thought keeps returning after a meeting.

The person wants to spend the evening doing something else, but their attention repeatedly returns to the conversation.

They open MindFlipOut.

Write down the thought.

Explore another perspective.

Close the app.

That may be enough.

In another situation, the person might give the thought another time to return through MindShoutOut.

They might place it in Thought Space alongside other thoughts.

They might associate it with Mental Steps and return to it when they want to take action.

They might spend some time with fewer stimuli using MindZoneOut.

Or they might decide that nothing further is needed.

These are alternative possibilities, not consecutive stages of a required process.

Every task begins as a thought, but not every thought is a task.

What MINDBEBOP Is — and Is Not

MINDBEBOP occupies a space between productivity software, mindfulness tools, digital wellbeing tools, and personal thought organization.

Its purpose is not limited to productivity, relaxation, or self-improvement.

It offers practical and experimental ways of interacting with thoughts and attention, depending on what a person wants or needs in a particular situation.

MINDBEBOP is:

  • A family of complementary everyday tools for thoughts and attention.
  • An approach that considers both external and internal demands on attention.
  • An interconnected architecture supporting different forms of thought interaction.
  • A system that can support organization, exploration, experimentation, and intentional movement.
  • Designed to let users decide what, if anything, happens next.
  • Designed to be available when useful, without requiring continued engagement.

MINDBEBOP is not:

  • Therapy, medical treatment, or crisis intervention.
  • A diagnostic or clinical assessment tool.
  • A replacement for professional mental health care.
  • A system that requires every thought to become a task or destination.
  • A system for scoring mental performance or determining how a person should think or feel.
  • A methodology that users must adopt in its entirety.

MINDBEBOP's effectiveness as a clinical intervention has not been established.

Its concepts and interactions are developed as practical and experimental tools, rather than as validated clinical treatments.

Different people may find different tools useful in different situations. Some may not find them useful at all.

MINDBEBOP does not require users to adopt a particular belief, philosophy, or interpretation of how the mind works.

Cross-Dimensional Handshake

Cross-Dimensional Handshake is MINDBEBOP's architecture for communication across the boundary between MINDBEBOP and the outside world.

The communication can travel in either direction.

Communication between MINDBEBOP apps on the same device is internal communication, not a Cross-Dimensional Handshake.

The defining boundary is between MINDBEBOP and the outside world.

Inbound

An inbound Cross-Dimensional Handshake brings a supported outside event into MINDBEBOP.

MindEvent represents MINDBEBOP's inbound Cross-Dimensional Handshake.

Outbound

An outbound Cross-Dimensional Handshake allows supported MINDBEBOP events to be sent as structured metadata to an external HTTPS destination explicitly configured by the user.

The external system determines what, if anything, happens next.

Outbound Cross-Dimensional Handshake follows a simple principle:

Events, not thoughts.

No user-created text is transmitted through outbound Cross-Dimensional Handshake.

This allows MINDBEBOP to remain modular while giving users a narrow, intentional boundary through which their own external systems can interact with supported MINDBEBOP events.

Explore Cross-Dimensional Handshake →

MindEvent

MindEvent is the inbound direction of Cross-Dimensional Handshake:

Outside World → MINDBEBOP

It provides a structured way for supported information or actions from outside MINDBEBOP to enter and initiate a supported MINDBEBOP experience.

This is distinct from ordinary communication between MINDBEBOP apps.

A local handoff from one MINDBEBOP app to another remains inside the system. MindEvent crosses the boundary from the outside world into MINDBEBOP.

The broader Cross-Dimensional Handshake architecture is not tied to a single transport technology.

MindEvent represents its inbound side, while supported outbound events provide the opposite direction.

Privacy & Data

MINDBEBOP is designed with a privacy-first approach. Personal thoughts and other user-created mental content remain under the user's control.

Communication among MINDBEBOP apps can happen locally as part of the Mental Background OS.

This internal app-to-app communication is not a Cross-Dimensional Handshake.

When the user enables an outbound Cross-Dimensional Handshake, a supported event can be sent to an external HTTPS destination explicitly configured by the user.

Outbound Cross-Dimensional Handshake transmits events, not thoughts.

Outbound events may contain limited event metadata, but no user-created text is transmitted through this mechanism.

MindEvent provides the inbound direction: supported communication from the outside world into MINDBEBOP.

Once information is sent to an external destination, that external system's own privacy practices apply.

Founder

Kentaro Yamauchi — Founder | Architect

MINDBEBOP began as a personal exploration of what to do with thoughts that continue asking for attention after the moment that produced them has passed.

Rather than building another productivity or wellness system, he began designing small tools for creating different relationships with those thoughts — giving them another perspective, another time, some distance, a place, or a possible path.

That exploration gradually expanded to include external stimulation, digital boundaries, spatial thought interaction, intentional mental movement, and the preservation of meaningful arrivals.

It evolved into Mental Background Detox and the interconnected architecture supporting it, Mental Background OS.

MINDBEBOP continues to explore how software can offer people more ways to interact with their minds without creating more things their minds have to do.
