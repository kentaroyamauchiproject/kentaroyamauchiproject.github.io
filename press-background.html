Press & Background

Press Summary

MINDBEBOP is a Mental Background OS — a modular system of lightweight tools designed to help people create an intentional relationship with thoughts that would otherwise be left unattended.

Rather than focusing on productivity, habit tracking, therapy, or self-optimization, MINDBEBOP focuses on the thoughts that remain in the Mental Background while attention is somewhere else. Some may behave like Thought Noise. Others may simply need somewhere to remain, another perspective, or eventually somewhere to go.

MINDBEBOP provides different ways of relating to those thoughts: looking at them differently, giving them another time to return, creating distance, giving attention room to move, giving thoughts persistent places through Spatial Mental Shelving, and beginning Mental Navigation when the user chooses a destination.

Thought Space extends this architecture by allowing selected thoughts to exist as persistent Thought Blocks in a spatial environment. A Thought Block does not have to become a task, goal, or destination. It can simply have a place and remain available until the user chooses to approach it again.

When there is somewhere the user wants to go from a thought or current state, Mental Navigation provides a way to move toward a Mental Destination. Mental Steps can provide a path, while allowing the route, next move, or destination to change along the way.

After a successful Mental Navigation, MindAnchor can preserve something meaningful from the state reached, making it available again after the immediate moment has passed.

Each MINDBEBOP tool has a narrowly defined role within this architecture. The system remains intentionally lightweight, non-clinical, private, and non-performative. Its tools are designed for intentional interaction rather than tracking, scoring, or maximizing time spent in the apps.

“Give a thought a place.
Choose how to relate to it.
Navigate when there is somewhere you want to go.”

MINDBEBOP explores what becomes possible when thoughts are not automatically treated as tasks to complete, problems to solve, or noise to eliminate.

The Architecture

MINDBEBOP is modular. Different moments can call for different relationships with a thought, and not every thought needs to move through the same sequence.

Reduce Thought Noise

Some thoughts behave like Thought Noise — continuing to draw attention when the user would rather their attention be somewhere else.

MINDBEBOP provides small tools for changing that relationship: looking at a recurring thought differently, giving it another time to return, creating distance from selected apps, or giving attention a quieter place to move. MINDBEBOP has described this part of the system as "Noise-Canceling Apps for Your Mind."

Give Thoughts Space

Not every thought needs to disappear, settle, or become an action. Some thoughts may simply benefit from having somewhere to exist outside immediate attention.

Thought Space allows selected thoughts to become persistent Thought Blocks that can be placed in a spatial environment. MINDBEBOP calls this Spatial Mental Shelving: making the act of consciously setting a thought aside spatial.

A thought can have a place before it has a destination.

Navigate

Sometimes there is somewhere the user wants to go from the current situation.

Mental Navigation is intentional movement from a Current Position toward a Mental Destination. Mental Steps can provide a path while allowing the route to adapt along the way. MINDBEBOP has described this idea as "Google Maps for Your Mind."

Mental Navigation is optional. A thought does not become a destination merely because it exists, and a possible path does not become an obligation merely because it is available.

Anchor

After a successful Mental Navigation, something about the state reached may be worth preserving.

MindAnchor provides an optional way to preserve something meaningful from that arrival so it can be encountered again later. MINDBEBOP has described this idea as "Bookmarks for Your Mind."

About MINDBEBOP

MINDBEBOP is a modular architecture for the Mental Background — the layer of everyday mental life where thoughts can remain present while attention is somewhere else.

Some of those thoughts may behave like Thought Noise. Sometimes changing the relationship with a thought is enough. Sometimes a thought remains and can simply be given a place. Sometimes the user decides there is somewhere they want to go from it.

MINDBEBOP provides lightweight tools across those possibilities: changing perspective, setting something aside, creating distance, giving attention room to move, placing thoughts through Spatial Mental Shelving, and supporting Mental Navigation through Mental Steps.

Rather than encouraging longer engagement, MINDBEBOP is designed to let the interaction end whenever the user no longer needs it. A thought does not need to be completed, cleared, scored, or resolved simply because it entered the system.

MINDBEBOP is intentionally non-clinical and non-performative. It is not designed to optimize people. It provides small, focused ways for people to choose how they want to relate to thoughts.

What MINDBEBOP Is — and Is Not

MINDBEBOP is:

  • A modular architecture for the Mental Background
  • Designed for intentional relationships with thoughts
  • A collection of lightweight, privacy-first tools
  • A way to reduce Thought Noise when a thought is behaving like noise
  • A spatial environment where selected thoughts can have persistent places
  • A Mental Navigation system for moments when the user chooses somewhere to go
  • Designed to preserve possibility without turning it into pressure

MINDBEBOP is not:

  • Therapy, medical treatment, or crisis intervention
  • A diagnostic or behavioral monitoring system
  • A productivity or performance optimization platform
  • A system that assumes every thought is noise, a problem, or a task
  • A complete map of everything in a person's mind
  • A social feed, habit-building, or engagement platform
  • A replacement for professional mental health care

Positioning

MINDBEBOP occupies a space adjacent to productivity software, mindfulness tools, journaling, and mental wellness apps, while approaching thoughts from a different direction.

Journaling can give a thought somewhere to be written. MINDBEBOP asks what kinds of intentional relationships can become possible after a thought has been noticed: another perspective, another time, distance, a place, a possible path, or Mental Navigation toward a chosen destination.

The system does not require every thought to be processed. A Thought Block can remain in Thought Space without becoming a task. Possible Mental Steps can remain connected to it without being started. A user can approach a thought, pass it by, move it, or leave it exactly where it is.

There are no feeds, scores, streaks, or requirements to clear everything. MINDBEBOP describes the wider architecture as the Mental Background OS.

Mental Background OS

MINDBEBOP describes its overall architecture as the Mental Background OS — a modular system designed for thoughts that remain present while attention is somewhere else.

The system provides different structures for different relationships: reducing Thought Noise, giving thoughts persistent places, changing perspective, supporting Mental Navigation, and preserving something meaningful from an arrival.

Each MINDBEBOP tool performs a narrowly defined role, allowing people to use only what makes sense for the moment rather than committing to a complete methodology, routine, or productivity system.

The Mental Background OS does not assume that every thought needs intervention. Sometimes the most appropriate relationship with a thought is simply allowing it to be there.

Thought Space and Spatial Mental Shelving

Thought Space is a spatial environment within MindBackyard where selected thoughts can exist as persistent Thought Blocks.

MINDBEBOP calls the underlying act Spatial Mental Shelving: giving a thought a visible, persistent place in space so it does not need to remain in immediate attention in order to remain available.

The “shelving” is not a literal shelf. It is the act of setting something aside mentally. Thought Space makes that relationship spatial.

Thought Blocks can be moved, approached, passed by, rotated, resized, and revisited. A Thought Block can also be connected with one or more possible sets of Mental Steps. Those paths remain optional.

Thought Space is not intended to visualize everything in the mind or automatically expose every association. The user chooses which thoughts, if any, belong there.

Mental Navigation

Mental Navigation is MINDBEBOP's concept for intentional movement from a Current Position toward a Mental Destination.

Mental Navigation begins when there is somewhere the user chooses to go. A thought itself is not automatically a destination, and the existence of a thought does not automatically begin Navigation.

Mental Steps can provide a route. Along the way, the next move, route, or even the Mental Destination can change. The destination can remain the same while the path adapts.

MINDBEBOP has described the concept as "Google Maps for Your Mind." The comparison refers to navigation: knowing where you are, choosing where you want to go, and allowing the route to change along the way.

Mental Navigation is one possible relationship with a thought, not the required outcome of every interaction with MINDBEBOP.

MindAnchor

A successful Mental Navigation may lead to a state or experience that the user wants to preserve.

MindAnchor provides an optional way to preserve something from that arrival so it can be encountered again later.

MINDBEBOP has described MindAnchor as "Bookmarks for Your Mind." It is not a score of progress and does not determine whether a Mental Navigation was successful.

MINDBEBOP is also exploring a larger question: whether something preserved from one successful Navigation might later change the perspective from which another Navigation begins. This is an area of exploration, not an assumption about how the mind works.

Cross-Dimensional Handshake

Cross-Dimensional Handshake is MINDBEBOP's architecture for communication across the boundary between MINDBEBOP and the outside world.

A Cross-Dimensional Handshake can be outbound or inbound. Communication between MINDBEBOP apps themselves is internal on-device communication and is not a Cross-Dimensional Handshake.

An outbound Cross-Dimensional Handshake allows supported MINDBEBOP events to become structured event information that can be sent to an external HTTPS destination configured by the user. Depending on the user's own external system, an event might participate in an automation, update another service, or trigger entirely custom behavior.

Outbound Cross-Dimensional Handshake transmits events, not thoughts. It does not transmit user-created text. Limited technical metadata can include an event identifier, timestamps, durations where relevant, and opaque identifiers associated with a Mental Steps run or session.

An inbound Cross-Dimensional Handshake allows supported information or actions from outside MINDBEBOP to enter the MINDBEBOP environment.

MINDBEBOP does not decide what an external system should do with an outbound event. The user chooses whether to configure the connection and which external destination to use.

MindEvent API

MindEvent is an inbound Cross-Dimensional Handshake.

It provides a way for supported information or actions from the outside world to enter MINDBEBOP and initiate a supported experience.

This is the inbound direction of the same broader Cross-Dimensional Handshake concept. In the other direction, supported MINDBEBOP events can cross outward to an external destination configured by the user.

MindEvent and outbound Cross-Dimensional Handshake do not turn ordinary communication between MINDBEBOP apps into external communication. Movement between MINDBEBOP apps remains internal to the MINDBEBOP environment.

Cross-Dimensional Handshake is not designed for mind reading, behavioral surveillance, analytics, or continuous tracking. It is a boundary mechanism that allows MINDBEBOP and user-chosen external systems to exchange supported events or actions in explicitly defined ways.

The Apps

MindEntry — The Mental Steps and Mental Navigation layer of MINDBEBOP, supporting movement from a Current Position toward a Mental Destination while allowing the route to adapt.

MindBackyard — Provides environments for attention and thoughts. Infinite Hallway gives attention somewhere to wander, while Thought Space lets selected thoughts have persistent places through Spatial Mental Shelving.

MindFlipOut — Gives recurring thoughts another perspective through a simple Heavy-to-Light interaction.

MindShoutOut — Lets a thought be given another time to return through reminders and related actions.

MindZoneOut — Provides short periods of intentional quiet and reduced stimulation.

MindBackOut — Uses Apple's Screen Time framework to create intentional distance from selected apps and can provide App Blocking during Mental Steps.

A Typical MINDBEBOP Interaction

A recurring thought appears after a meeting.

The user opens MindFlipOut.

Types the thought.

Looks at it from another perspective.

Closes the app.

The thought may feel different, or it may remain. Nothing requires the user to continue.

If the thought remains and the user wants to keep it somewhere, it could later become a Thought Block in Thought Space. If there is somewhere the user wants to go from it, it could instead become the beginning of Mental Navigation.

The relationship is intentional, but the outcome is not predetermined.

Founder

Kentaro Yamauchi — Founder | Architect
MINDBEBOP began as a personal exploration of the thoughts that continue asking for attention while everyday life is already moving on. Rather than building another productivity or wellness app, he began designing small tools for creating different relationships with those thoughts.

That exploration gradually evolved into the Mental Background OS — a modular architecture encompassing Thought Noise, Spatial Mental Shelving, Mental Navigation, and other ways of intentionally interacting with thoughts in the Mental Background.

Privacy & Data

MINDBEBOP is designed with a privacy-first approach. Core thought-related content and processing are designed to remain primarily on the user's device.

Communication between MINDBEBOP apps on the device is internal MINDBEBOP communication and is not Cross-Dimensional Handshake.

Cross-Dimensional Handshake applies when communication crosses the boundary between MINDBEBOP and the outside world. It can operate in either direction. MindEvent is an inbound Cross-Dimensional Handshake.

Outbound Cross-Dimensional Handshake transmits supported event metadata, not thoughts. User-created text is not included in outbound Cross-Dimensional Handshake events.

External communication occurs only through supported features that the user chooses to configure or use. For complete details, see the MINDBEBOP Privacy Policy.
