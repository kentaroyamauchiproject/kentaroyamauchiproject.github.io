Welcome.

Your mind is not just a place where thoughts appear. It is where your everyday life unfolds.

MINDBEBOP is a family of noise-canceling apps for your mind that gives you different ways to interact with your thoughts and attention.

Throughout the day, your attention moves between work, family, conversations, memories, responsibilities, ideas, entertainment and things that have not happened yet.

Some of these movements are intentional. Others happen without you noticing.

You may be sitting at home while your mind is still at work. You may be working while your mind is somewhere else. You may be thinking about something important at a time when there is nothing you can do about it.

The difficulty is not necessarily the thought itself. Sometimes it is the relationship between the thought, your attention and what you want to be doing at that moment.

What if you had more ways to interact with what is on your mind?

That is the idea behind MINDBEBOP.

No streaks. No scores. No pressure to process everything.

Every task begins as a thought.

But not every thought is a task.

A thought can be something you need to do, something you want to explore, something you remember, something you fear, or something you simply want to keep.

A To Do app helps you keep track of what needs to be done. A calendar helps you organize when things need to happen.

These are useful tools. But there are other aspects of your mental life that do not necessarily fit into a checklist or a schedule.

You may have several projects competing for your attention. You may know what needs to be done but find it difficult to begin. You may want to explore an idea without immediately turning it into an action. You may want to put something aside without forgetting it.

MINDBEBOP gives you ways to work with both actionable and non-actionable thoughts.

You can organize tasks, develop projects, explore ideas, reconsider perspectives, prepare for action or simply give something a place.

A thought can be something to act on, something to work with, or simply something to have.

More than one way to interact with a thought.

When something occupies your attention, you do not necessarily have to choose between continuing to think about it and trying to stop thinking about it.

There are other possibilities.

Another perspective.
Look at the same thought differently without denying what happened.

Another time.
Decide when you want a thought to return instead of repeatedly reminding yourself about it.

Another place.
Give a thought a visible place alongside other thoughts, tasks, ideas and projects.

Some distance.
Create a temporary boundary between yourself and digital distractions.

Some quiet.
Stop introducing new information for a while and give your existing thoughts room to settle.

A direction.
Take small, manageable steps toward somewhere you want to be.

These possibilities are available through different MINDBEBOP tools. You can use them independently or bring them together when it is useful.

The same thought may need something different at a different moment.

Thought Space

A spatial environment for your thoughts, tasks, projects and ideas.

Imagine trying to live in a home without shelves, drawers, rooms or furniture.

Everything would have to exist in the same space, without much separation or organization.

Something similar can happen when many different things occupy your mind at the same time.

In MindBackyard's Thought Space, you can turn thoughts into persistent Thought Blocks and arrange them in a three-dimensional environment.

A Thought Block can represent anything on your mind:

  • A task you need to complete.
  • A project you are developing.
  • An idea you want to explore.
  • A responsibility you want to organize.
  • A memory you want to keep.
  • A concern you want to work with.
  • Something you have not decided what to do with yet.

Move your Thought Blocks. Rotate them. Resize them. Place related thoughts near each other. Create relationships between them. Arrange them in ways that make sense to you.

You can also create Nested Thought Spaces within Thought Blocks.

A Thought Block representing a project, for example, can contain another Thought Space with its own tasks, ideas, concerns and plans.

A Thought Space can become a workspace, a planning environment, a collection of ideas, a personal archive or a place to experiment with your thoughts.

It does not have to be organized in a particular way.

Thought Furniture

Your Thought Space can also contain Thought Furniture — objects designed to offer different ways of interacting with Thought Blocks.

Rather than simply organizing a thought, you can explore it, examine it from another perspective or work with it through a guided process.

Different pieces of Thought Furniture offer different possibilities.

You can also leave a Thought Block on a piece of Thought Furniture without doing anything with it immediately.

From a thought to a path

A Thought Block can connect to one or more sets of Mental Steps.

For example, a Thought Block representing a project might connect to Mental Steps for getting started, reviewing progress or deciding what to do next.

A Thought Block representing a concern might connect to Mental Steps for exploring that concern or taking a practical action.

You can also create a new set of Mental Steps directly from a Thought Block.

This allows you to move between organizing your thoughts and doing something with them.

Thought Space is not just somewhere to put your thoughts. It is somewhere to work with them.

Mental Navigation

From where you are now to where you want to be.

Think about how you use a GPS.

You identify where you are, choose where you want to go and follow a route. If something changes along the way, the route can change too.

Mental Navigation applies a similar idea to your mental life.

Your Current Position is where you actually are now — not where you think you should be.

Your Mental Destination (Point B) is where you want to go.

It might be starting a task, making a decision, leaving work behind, changing your perspective, preparing for a conversation or simply becoming ready to do something.

The distance between your Current Position and your Mental Destination may feel small or large.

You do not necessarily have to cover that distance all at once.

Mental Steps

Mental Steps are small, structured actions that can help you move toward your Mental Destination.

A Mental Step might involve:

  • Answering a question.
  • Writing down a thought.
  • Exploring another perspective.
  • Taking a mental break.
  • Temporarily blocking distracting apps.
  • Getting up and walking outside.
  • Washing one dish.
  • Opening a document and writing one sentence.
  • Taking another small action in the real world.

In MindEntry, Mental Steps are presented one step at a time.

A route can include other MINDBEBOP apps, real-world actions or both.

You can create and save your own sets of Mental Steps for situations you encounter repeatedly.

You can also connect them to Thought Blocks in Thought Space, making them available whenever you want to work with those thoughts.

Mental Navigation is not about following a fixed path regardless of what happens. Your Current Position may change as you move, and your route can change with it.

You choose your Mental Destination. You only need to see your next step.

MindAnchor

Keep something from an arrival worth remembering.

Sometimes you reach a Mental Destination and discover something useful along the way.

Perhaps you found a way to get started when starting felt difficult. Perhaps you discovered another perspective on a recurring thought. Perhaps something helped you leave work behind and enjoy the rest of your day.

The next time you encounter a similar situation, you may not immediately remember what helped you before.

MindAnchor gives you a way to preserve something meaningful from an arrival.

It might be a thought, a realization, a perspective or something that helped you move forward.

Rather than having to rediscover it every time, you can make it available to yourself again.

MindAnchor does not require you to recreate exactly the same experience. It simply gives you something familiar to return to when it is useful.

Something discovered today may become useful again tomorrow.

MindBlank

Sometimes nothing needs to come next.

Much of today's software is designed around engagement.

How often you return. How long you stay. How much you interact. How much content you consume.

MINDBEBOP does not assume that more interaction is always better.

You might open a MINDBEBOP app, use it for a few seconds and return to what you were doing.

You might spend time with your thoughts in Thought Space or sit quietly with MindZoneOut.

You might not need another tool at all.

MindBlank looks in the other direction from conventional engagement measurements: time spent away from active MINDBEBOP interaction in supported situations.

It does not claim to know whether your mind was literally blank or what you were thinking during that time.

It is not a score, streak, diagnosis or performance metric. There is no target you need to reach.

You do not have to turn every moment into an activity.

Mental Background OS

Each MINDBEBOP app has its own purpose.

You can use any of them independently, without needing to install or use the others.

But they can also work together.

The architecture connecting these tools and concepts is called Mental Background OS.

It is not an operating system that replaces the one on your device. It is a connected environment for interacting with your thoughts and attention.

A thought can move between different tools depending on what you want to do with it.

For example:

Working on a project

Organize your project as Thought Blocks in MindBackyard.

Connect a Thought Block to a set of Mental Steps in MindEntry.

Use MindBackOut to temporarily block distracting apps while you work.

If something unrelated comes to mind, use MindShoutOut to let it return later.

Continue with your project.

Leaving work behind

Begin a set of Mental Steps in MindEntry with the Mental Destination of enjoying your evening.

Give unfinished responsibilities another time through MindShoutOut.

Explore another perspective on an unpleasant thought through MindFlipOut.

Spend a few quiet moments with MindZoneOut.

Return to your evening.

These are examples, not prescribed routines.

You can create different routes, use different combinations of tools or simply use one app for a single moment.

The tools are connected. How you use them is up to you.

Reduce. Navigate. Anchor.

MINDBEBOP brings these different possibilities together through three broad concepts.

Reduce.
Reduce unnecessary demands on your attention. Give thoughts another perspective, another time or another place. Create distance from distractions or give your mind some quiet.

Navigate.
When there is somewhere you want to go, use Mental Steps to move from your Current Position toward your Mental Destination.

Anchor.
When you arrive somewhere meaningful, preserve something from that arrival through MindAnchor.

These are not three stages that you must complete in order.

You may want to reduce distractions before working on a project. You may want to organize your thoughts without navigating anywhere. You may want to begin a set of Mental Steps immediately. You may want to return to something you previously anchored.

Or you may not need any of them right now.

Possibility is not pressure.

Beyond Digital Detox

Digital Detox recognizes that constantly receiving new information can affect how we spend our attention.

But the information we receive can continue to occupy our minds long after we put our devices away.

And not everything that occupies our attention comes from a digital device.

Responsibilities, memories, expectations, fears, desires, unfinished tasks and future plans can all remain active in the mental background.

MINDBEBOP calls its approach to reducing unnecessary mental noise and friction Mental Background Detox.

It is not about emptying your mind, eliminating thoughts or treating every thought as a problem.

It is about having practical ways to interact with what occupies your attention, whether you want to organize it, explore it, act on it, return to it later or leave it alone.

MINDBEBOP is not a diagnostic tool or a form of therapy. It is a family of everyday tools for interacting with your thoughts and attention.

Where do you begin?

You do not need to understand every MINDBEBOP concept before using an app.

You do not need to build a Thought Space, create a set of Mental Steps or establish a routine.

Start with something that is already part of your life.

A project you want to organize. A thought you want to look at differently. Something you want to remember later. A distraction you want to step away from. Somewhere you want to go.

Or simply a moment when you want a little quiet.
