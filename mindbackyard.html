MindBackyard

App size: ~3.1 MB
Available on the App Store

Your mind wants to move.

Sometimes your attention needs somewhere to wander.

Sometimes a thought needs somewhere to be.

MindBackyard gives you space for both.

Wander through low-stimulation environments without needing to accomplish anything, or enter Thought Space and give thoughts persistent places of their own.

What is MindBackyard?

MindBackyard is a place for attention and thoughts when neither needs another stream of information.

You can wander through simple environments, interact with tactile visual noise, or enter Thought Space and place thoughts into a persistent 3D environment.

There are no scores, streaks, or pressure to clear everything away.

Sometimes you move. Sometimes the thought moves. Sometimes you leave it exactly where it is.

Two Ways to Use the Backyard

MindBackyard currently explores two different relationships with attention.

Wander

Enter environments such as Infinite Hallway and give restless attention somewhere simple to move.

There is no destination and nothing you need to accomplish.

Thought Space

Give a thought a persistent place in 3D space.

Move it, rotate it, change its size, move closer or farther away, leave it somewhere, and return when you want.

A Thought Block does not have to become a task.

A thought can have a place

before it has a destination.

Spatial Mental Shelving

Sometimes you do not need to solve a thought.

You just do not want to keep carrying it in your immediate attention.

Thought Space lets you turn that thought into a Thought Block and give it somewhere to remain.

MINDBEBOP calls this Spatial Mental Shelving.

The "shelving" is not a literal shelf.

It is the act of setting a thought aside while keeping it available.

Thought Space makes that relationship spatial.

I do not need to keep thinking about this.

I know where it is.

No category is required. No priority is required. No deadline is required.

The thought can simply have a place.

How Thought Space Works

  1. Create or bring a thought into Thought Space.
  2. It becomes a persistent Thought Block.
  3. Place it somewhere in the 3D space.
  4. Move, rotate, resize, or reposition it whenever you want.
  5. Leave it there without needing to do anything about it.
  6. Return when you choose to approach it again.

The thought does not have to keep coming to you.

You can choose when to go to the thought.

Connect Mental Steps to a Thought

Sometimes giving a thought a place is enough.

Sometimes you realize there is somewhere you might want to go from it.

A Thought Block can be connected to one or more saved Mental Steps in MindEntry.

When Mental Steps are connected, a link indicator on the Thought Block shows how many are available.

Tap the indicator to see the Mental Steps connected to that thought.

Choose one, and MindEntry opens it so you can begin.

Connecting Mental Steps does not turn the Thought Block into a task.

Nothing begins automatically.

Several possible paths can remain connected at the same time.

And you do not have to take any of them.

Possibility without pressure.

A Thought Is Not a Destination

A Thought Block represents a thought.

It does not have to represent a goal.

A thought such as Family, Health, or Finance may have several possible directions.

Different Mental Steps may lead toward different Mental Destinations.

Or you may simply leave the Thought Block where it is without navigating anywhere.

The thought can remain.

The possibilities can remain.

You decide whether to move.

Common Moments

You finished one thing but are not ready to begin the next.

Your hands keep reaching for your phone even though you do not want more content.

Your mind feels restless, but thinking harder is not helping.

A thought keeps occupying attention, but you do not want to deal with it right now.

You want to put something aside without losing it.

You want to return to a thought on your terms rather than have it keep returning to you.

You can see several possible directions from a thought but do not want to choose one yet.

You want to move through something without needing to arrive anywhere.

The Backyard Environments

Different parts of MindBackyard provide different ways for attention to move.

  • Infinite Hallway: Wander through a minimal repeating environment without needing to arrive anywhere.
  • Low-Stakes Doors: Open one door and find another. Keep moving without needing to choose the correct one.
  • Perspective Room: Shift the way a space appears without solving or completing anything.
  • Tactile Noise: Tap, touch, and interact with particles, ripples, and subtle haptics.
  • Thought Space: Give thoughts persistent places, practice Spatial Mental Shelving, and keep possible Mental Steps available when there is somewhere you may want to go.

Low-Stimulation by Design

MindBackyard is intentionally simple.

It is not designed to excite you, reward you, or maximize the amount of time you spend inside the app.

It offers movement, space, and quiet interaction for moments when your attention wants activity without another flood of information.

Thought Space follows the same principle.

Thought Blocks do not need alerts, deadlines, completion states, or constant demands for attention.

The empty space matters too.

A Buffer Between Mental States

Sometimes the mind does not move directly from one state into another.

Work does not instantly become rest. Overload does not instantly become focus. A looping thought does not instantly become silence.

MindBackyard can sit between those states and give your attention time and space to shift.

When Wandering Becomes Part of a Route

Wandering does not always have to be aimless.

MindBackyard can also become one part of Mental Steps running in MindEntry.

A Mental Step may send you into MindBackyard for a short buffer, some movement, or a change of perspective.

When you are ready, you can return to the same active Mental Steps and continue.

In other words, MindBackyard can work in both directions.

A Thought Block can lead you into Mental Steps.

And Mental Steps can lead you into MindBackyard.

Both can become part of MINDBEBOP Mental Navigation.

Thought Space Is Not a Productivity Board

Thought Space is not a 3D task manager.

There are no To Do, Doing, and Done columns.

A Thought Block does not become unfinished work merely because it exists.

Connecting Mental Steps does not create an obligation to complete them.

And the goal is not to empty Thought Space.

A thought can be present

without demanding action.

Aimless Has a Place

Not every thought needs to be solved. Not every moment needs to become productive. Not every movement needs a destination.

Sometimes attention simply needs somewhere gentle to move.

Sometimes a thought simply needs somewhere to be.

MindBackyard provides space for both.

What We're Exploring Next

Thought Space raises a new question:

If a thought can have a place,

what else could change the way we encounter it?

MINDBEBOP calls one area of this exploration Thought Furniture.

Thought Furniture is an experimental idea for structures and interactions that could change the relationship between you and a Thought Block.

A structure might reveal something. Conceal something. Change perspective. Combine thoughts. Separate them. Or make a possible direction easier to notice.

The goal is not to fill Thought Space with virtual furniture.

The question is:

What relationship with a thought

could this interaction make possible?

Thought Furniture is still an experiment.

Learn more about Thought Space, Spatial Mental Shelving, and what we're exploring next.
